Banishment

Banish the traitors before they murder everyone. Banishment is a free game like Mafia and Werewolf that you play on your phones, with no moderator: the game deals the roles, counts the votes and keeps the night secret.

Rather learn by playing? Try a round against bots, with a guide explaining each step as it happens.

Players and roles

Banishment is made for 5 to 10 players, each with their own phone or computer. With 3 or 4, bots take the empty seats. Each player secretly learns their role. Most are faithful. One is a traitor with 5 or 6 players, two with 7 to 9, and three with 10. The traitors know each other; the faithful don't know anyone.

How a game goes

  1. The round table. Each day, everyone argues about who the traitors are, then votes to banish one player. Votes are secret until everyone has voted, then the table sees who voted for whom.
    • The banished player's role is revealed: faithful or traitor.
    • A tie gets one revote between the tied players. If it ties again, nobody is banished.
  2. Night. The traitors choose a faithful player to murder. Everyone else waits, and learns who died in the morning.
    • If a traitor was banished that day and others remain, they recruit a faithful player to their side instead. Nobody dies that night, and only the traitors know who switched.
  3. The endgame. Once four or fewer remain, each day starts with a vote to end the game. If everyone votes to end it, the game is over: the traitors win if any of them is still in, and the faithful win if not. One vote to keep going sends the table back to banish another player.

Winning

  • The faithful win when every traitor is banished.
  • The traitors win once they can no longer be outvoted: when there are as many traitors left as faithful.

Banished and murdered players keep watching. They see who the traitors are, but they can't vote or tell anyone.

Banishment, Mafia and the TV show

If you have played Mafia or Werewolf, you know the rhythm: accuse by day, murder by night. Banishment adds two twists from the TV show The Traitors: recruitment, and the endgame vote, where the last few players have to decide whether they trust each other enough to stop.

Traitors is an unofficial fan game, not affiliated with the TV show or any game publisher.

Prefer missions to murders? Try Sabotage, where nobody is eliminated.
